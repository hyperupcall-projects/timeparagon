# Reminders

Both apps can remind you of what is coming up. Turn them on under
**Settings → Reminders**. They are off until you do.

Each kind of item on the calendar has its own switch, lead time and sound:
Events, Tasks, Time Blocks, Habits, Sleep and Travel. Travel reminders tell you
when to leave, using the trip times worked out for the day; trips without a
time yet get no reminder.

For events, **Use each event's Google Calendar reminders** follows the
notification reminders set on the event in Google Calendar (or the
calendar's defaults) instead of the lead time. Google Calendar's own app
will remind you of the same events, so turn one of them off if you get two.

Only what is visible gets reminded: a hidden calendar or TimeSlay source is not
reminded either. All-day items, finished tasks and undated tasks are skipped.

## Sounds

- **Device default** plays whatever the device uses for notifications.
- **Silent** shows the notification without a sound.
- **Chime, Bell, Beep, Marimba, Soft Pulse, Ding, Ding Dong, Rise, Fall,
  Kalimba, Glass, Knock and Bubble** are generated by TimeSlay.
- The device's own notification sounds are listed after those.

Choosing a sound plays it on the desktop. **Test** posts a sample reminder.

## Desktop

Reminders are freedesktop notifications posted by the side panel instance
while it runs. Sounds play through PipeWire at the alarm volume. The device
sounds are the files in installed sound themes (`/usr/share/sounds/*/stereo`);
the default is the current theme's `message-new-instant`, read from
`kdeglobals` on KDE, falling back to the `freedesktop` theme. When no theme is
installed, the notification daemon is asked to play its own.

## Android

Reminders are scheduled with `AlarmManager`, so they arrive with the app
closed, and are re-armed after a reboot or update. The next 36 hours are planned
whenever the app loads a day and in the widget's background sync, which also
fetches tomorrow while reminders are on.

Each kind has its own notification channel, so it can be adjusted in Android's
notification settings too. Android fixes a channel's sound when it is created,
so changing the sound in TimeSlay replaces that kind's channel. Android 13 and
later ask for permission to post notifications when reminders are turned on.

The generated sounds are rendered into the APK's `res/raw` at build time by
`cargo run -p timeslay-core --example render_sounds`.

## Settings

Reminder settings are shared between devices (see
[Settings](./data-model.md#settings)). The two switches are rows of `settings`:

| Key | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `reminders.enabled` | `false` | The master switch |
| `reminders.use_calendar_reminders` | `true` | Use each event's Google reminders instead of the `events` lead time |

Each kind of reminder is a row of the `reminders` table. A kind with no row
uses its defaults (enabled, with 10 minutes for events, 30 for sleep, 5 for
travel and 0 for the rest, and the device's default sound):

| `category` | `enabled` | `minutes_before` | `sound` (JSON) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `events` | 1 | 10 | `{"kind":"default"}` |
| `tasks` | 1 | 0 | `{"kind":"tone","tone":"kalimba"}` |
| `sleep` | 1 | 30 | `{"kind":"device","name":"Ocean · Bell","uri":"/usr/share/sounds/ocean/stereo/bell.oga"}` |
| `habits` | 1 | 0 | `{"kind":"silent"}` |

`category` is one of `events`, `tasks`, `time_blocks`, `habits`, `sleep` and
`travel`.
